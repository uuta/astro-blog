I had a job interview in English for the first time in a year and a half. It was 90% Japanese, 10% English. The company introduction and my self-pitch, which took up most of the time, were in Japanese, and then I answered three questions in English to check my speaking ability. The result was a crushing defeat. The questions were as ordinary as it gets. "How did you study English?" "What's your ideal job?" "What do you do in your free time?" Those three. The interviewer spoke fluently, and I just could not understand the second and third questions. In the end I understood the second one by asking again. But the third one went completely over my head, and the interviewer changed the question.

To redeem myself, I picked up scraps of words and tried to speak, and what was waiting for me was a spark in my brain. I didn't know what I was saying myself. It felt deeply embarrassing. Five-year-old-level English exploded out of me. Make a list of likely questions, shadow them over and over. Blow up the picture in your head beforehand, walk into the interview fully prepared. The hazy picture of success I'd blown up popped beautifully when it counted. The groundless confidence that had swelled over the past year, "maybe I'm actually good at my job", was washed away completely by a very well-grounded awkwardness.

What on earth were my three years of studying English for? Instead of adding the English words I came across on Netflix to Anki, should I have been talking to people around the world more on DMM Eikaiwa? Was it a mistake to blow off DMM Eikaiwa's Daily News because preparing for it was a pain? A voice echoes in my head: dysfunctional in both quality and quantity. It feels like a kick to a body that's already falling apart with age. My crotch is the only thing that gets to be dysfunctional.

Brilliant software engineers keep putting their stuff out on Twitter, blogs and books. People on the front line. If they can do it, maybe I can too. That kind of reckless confidence has made history, and has vanished without a sound. Readers project themselves onto the author and copy them. It's like a kid who watches Arnold Schwarzenegger as John Kruger in *Eraser*, recklessly shooting it up in enemy territory, and then starts acting cocky with his friends. I need a strategy as a ★★★☆☆ or ★★★★☆ person, not a ★★★★★ one. What's missing can't be filled easily, after all. That's what I'm thinking as I eat matcha pudding.
