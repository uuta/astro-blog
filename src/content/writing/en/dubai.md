Dubai is unbelievably hot. Shade is hard to find, and walk a little and you're exposed to murderous direct sunlight. Sharjah has no metro, so you can't get around intuitively. If I'd called a taxi on Careem, the 14 km trip from Sharjah to Dubai would have cost 20,000 yen lol

Meanwhile, the snack I had, chicken tikka wrapped in roti, kind of like a KFC Twister, was 6AED (240 yen). With some effort you can live cheaply, but right next to that lies ridiculously expensive luxury, is how it feels.



Apparently Sharjah and Dubai are completely different countries. Dubai is one of the countries in the United Arab Emirates (UAE) to begin with, so it's not Dubai = UAE. Hmm, complicated. A staff member at the hotel I stayed at asked me "Are China and Japan different?", and I thought, well, obviously, but when a place is geographically far away, the outline of the information gets blurry. It's the same as Japanese people not being able to tell you the difference between India and Pakistan.

## Dubai, like Africa

It'd be a waste to stay only in Sharjah, so I went to the central area of Dubai where the metro lines cross.



Is this Africa...? Isn't Dubai supposed to be ridiculously huge towers next to man-made beaches, with nobles in white robes strutting around? I walked to the hotel bewildered by a scene like Africa plus India, divided by two. That the busy, cluttered area where metro stations intersect is taken over by something chaotic and African/Indian might be a sight unique to Dubai, where 90% of the population is foreign.

## Brutal heat is the worst, and if you get a tan you lose

Heat is extremely harmful. People in the Middle East seem to come alive at night, and I think that's because the days are too hot. Get hit by direct sunlight and you lose, sweat and you lose, work and you lose. The rich upper crust in white robes strutting around Dubai Mall aren't on the packed trains at rush hour. The cheap metro is the main artery for moving workers efficiently. Packed trains are the worst to ride in every country.



Of course Tokyo is the worst. People still have to get on packed trains there because work is one of the ways to show your class. Japan doesn't have royalty or anything. Your company, where you belong and your income are the tools that show your class. Fundamentally, nothing has changed. Work is the only tool that gives you an identity.
