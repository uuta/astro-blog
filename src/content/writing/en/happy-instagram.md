How are we supposed to fight isolation by algorithm? With the rise of AI driving down the value of things humans create, most people are now consumers. Diaries have lost their value, and videos, which used to be valuable as firsthand evidence, can no longer be judged real or fake. Anyone's individual experience can be imitated easily. Rules of thumb and knowledge are no longer an advantage. The pastoral age isn't coming back. Something has died.

We're ruled by algorithms. Features are quietly developed and reworked to stretch the time we spend in apps. Recently, Instagram made it easy to see the reels other people have liked. A garbage update, the worst. A little secret pleasure, like snacks with a drink, is now always on display. Meta is a blatant company that invades privacy without consent.

One of the big problems is that they're holding even our friendships hostage. The order of Stories and Feed is set by an algorithm they decided on their own. Through DMs, Instagram itself became part of how we communicate, one more piece of infrastructure you can't easily give up. It builds close relationships, and sometimes smashes them to pieces. Destructive changes to the algorithm trample stable relationships. Friendships that should be solid get blown away easily — it's obvious from the fact that the class reunions that live on in our parents' generation never happen at all in ours.

Friendships die far too easily. For the past few years I've been forcing my way through life doing things beyond my means, and that has constantly required building new relationships.
