"Your entry stamp has expired. You're on overstay."

That came from the real estate agent handling my room. That's bananas. I'd paid 1,900 baht and my application to extend my stay was supposedly approved, yet immigration apparently considered me an illegal overstayer. Why......

"Anyway, go there yourself."

With that one LINE message, it was decided that the first day of my winter break would be spent at the immigration office.

Right away, I took Bolt, a Thai taxi app, to the immigration office. In an office packed mostly with farang (white people) living in Thailand, I got the impression the staff were working properly and strictly. I'd pictured grinning officials with bribes and under-the-table money everywhere, so when I saw them explaining things logically with stern faces, I was almost moved.

To cut to the chase, I wasn't on overstay. When you apply for a visa exemption online, they put a sticker in your passport showing your permitted stay. This procedure is fairly new, so an official probably made the wrong call. It can only be a guess, because after I went around the counters as the staff told me to, my extension was at some point recognized as valid. Nobody takes responsibility; it just gets processed without fuss. The mai pen rai system.

I wonder which is better, Japan or Thailand. Japan, which pushes its corporate philosophy top-down all the way to the bottom of the ladder, or Thailand, where for better or worse individuals are given their own discretion. I can picture a Japanese person apologizing from the bottom of their heart to a complainer who's completely lost it, while the Thai staff just went on with the paperwork, no apology, no reflection.

Someone made a mistake, so I had to go all the way there myself, and yet the system wraps it up with an unspoken understanding and no one to blame. Pretty interesting, isn't it... They sometimes say "Mistaaa, easy easy", but if you don't get hold of the firsthand facts properly yourself, in the end you're the one who gets badly hurt.
